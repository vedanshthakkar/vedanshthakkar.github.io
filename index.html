About Me

My research interests center on four interconnected problems in healthcare AI: longitudinal representation learning from clinical narratives, grounded & interpretable clinical report generation, equitable AI across diverse patient populations and institutions, and multimodal fusion. The thread connecting all four is a single observation: AI systems fail in deployment not primarily because of modeling limitations, but because they are designed for static, single-institution, single-modality settings that do not reflect the longitudinal, heterogeneous, and high-stakes nature of real clinical care.

Previously, I led clinical AI projects at the University of Minnesota Department of Surgery advised by Dr. Christopher J. Tignanelli, with co-advisors Greg Silverman and Dr. Nicholas Ingraham, including an AI system that automates activation of an evidence-based clinical decision support pathway for rib fracture patients, deployed in production at M Health Fairview , and longitudinal symptom severity tracking for vagal nerve stimulation patients. Prior to that, I worked at TechMah Medical under Dr. Emam Fatah and Dr. Mohamed R. Mahfouz on deep learning for surgical planning, and at Neocortex Robotics under Dr. Goutham Mallapragada on warehouse automation AI. I completed my M.S. in Electrical Engineering at Vanderbilt University advised by Dr. Alan Peters where my research covered geometric camera calibration and stereo 3D reconstruction from first principles, and a systematic empirical study of CNN architectures for image recognition tasks.

I have also served as a peer reviewer for the JAMIA Open 2025.

I am currently working as a Data Scientist at Yash Technologies where I am building Agentic AI systems at enterprise level and looking for PhD opportunities in Biomedical Informatics and Computer Science for Fall 2027. If you would like to discuss research or collaborate, feel free to reach out.

Clinical NLP · University of Minnesota · PLOS ONE 2025
A comparative analysis of large language models versus traditional information extraction methods for real-world evidence of patient symptomatology in acute and post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2
Background
Patient symptoms, crucial for disease progression and diagnosis, are often captured in unstructured clinical notes. Large language models (LLMs) offer potential advantages in extracting patient symptoms compared to traditional rule-based information extraction (IE) systems.
Methods
This study compared fine-tuned LLMs (LLaMA2-13B and LLaMA3-8B) against BioMedICUS, a rule-based IE system, for extracting symptoms related to acute and post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 from clinical notes. The study utilized three corpora: UMN-COVID, UMN-PASC, and N3C-COVID. Prevalence, keyword and fairness analyses were conducted to assess symptom distribution and model equity across demographics.
Results
BioMedICUS outperformed fine-tuned LLMs in most cases. On the UMN PASC data set, BioMedICUS achieved a macro-averaged F1-score of 0.70 for positive mention detection, compared to 0.66 for LLaMA2-13B and 0.62 for LLaMA3-8B. For the N3C COVID dataset, BioMedICUS scored 0.75, while LLaMA2-13B and LLaMA3-8B scored 0.53 and 0.68, respectively for positive mention detection. However, LLMs performed better in specific instances, such as detecting positive mentions of change in sleep in the UMN PASC dataset, where LLaMA2-13B (0.79) and LLaMA3-8B (0.65) outperformed BioMedICUS (0.60). For fairness analysis, BioMedICUS generally showed stronger performance across patient demographics. Keyword analysis using ANOVA on symptom distributions across all three corpora showed that both corpus and symptom have a statistically significant effect on log-transformed term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) values such that corpus accounts for 52% of the variance in log_tfidf values and symptom accounts for 35%.
Conclusion
While BioMedICUS generally outperformed the LLMs, the latter showed promising results in specific areas, particularly LLaMA3-8B, in identifying negative symptom mentions. However, both LLaMA models faced challenges in demographic fairness and generalizability. These findings underscore the need for diverse, high-quality training datasets and robust annotation processes to enhance LLMs’ performance and reliability in clinical applications.

Clinical NLP · University of Minnesota · Under Review at JAMIA
LLM-Based Longitudinal Symptom Severity Tracking in Vagus Nerve Stimulation Patients: A Two-Stage Pipeline with Explainable AI
Background & Objective
Longitudinal symptom tracking in vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) patients relies on periodic surveys and standardized rating scales, limiting scalability and temporal resolution. As part of the NIH-funded REVEAL center (U54AT012307), this study develops and validates a two-stage large language model (LLM) pipeline to automate symptom classification and severity assessment from unstructured clinical notes, producing the first structured longitudinal symptom severity dataset for VNS patients.
Methods
We analyzed 1,427 clinical notes from 95 patients across five corpora. LLaMA models (3.2-1B, 3.2-3B, 3.3-70B) were fine-tuned via parameter-efficient fine-tuning to classify 13 VNS-relevant symptoms as present, absent, or negated (Stage 1). The best-performing model assessed severity as mild, moderate, or severe using generated knowledge prompting without additional fine-tuning (Stage 2). Longitudinal analysis tracked quarterly symptom trajectories in 23 VNS patients via Kaplan-Meier modeling. Equity analyses across racial and gender subgroups and SHAP-based xAI inter-symptom modeling were conducted to assess fairness and interpretability.
Results
LLaMA-3.3-70B achieved F1-scores of 0.98 (95% CI, 0.96-0.99) for seizures and 0.99 (95% CI, 0.98-1.00) for self-harm. Severity F1-scores ranged from 0.76 to 0.96, with the majority of symptom severities exceeding 0.80. Model size was the dominant performance predictor (29.4% variance explained), with no significant corpus effect, indicating cross-domain generalizability. Gender equity was consistent across symptom classification, with precision of 0.95 for both male and female patients. Longitudinal analysis identified a 59-day median time to seizure improvement post-VNS procedure, with a representative patient demonstrating 100% reduction in seizure severity. SHAP analysis revealed clinically coherent inter-symptom dependencies, including bidirectional depression-anxiety relationships and seizure-sleep disorder comorbidity.
Discussion and Conclusion
This pipeline reliably automates symptom extraction and longitudinal severity modeling from real-world clinical notes. The primary deliverable, fulfilling REVEAL's Data and Analysis Core Aim 2, is the first structured longitudinal symptom severity dataset in VNS patients, enabling future treatment response prediction, personalized device programming, and a reproducible benchmark for clinical natural language processing (NLP) in neuromodulation research.

Medical Imaging · University of Minnesota · Under Review at Trauma Surgery & Acute Care Open
Development, External Validation, and Clinical Deployment of a Multimodal AI Model for Automated Activation of Evidence-Based Rib Fracture Care
Background
A clinical decision support (CDS) system for rib fracture care reduces mortality by 40% and unplanned ICU transfers by 70% but reaches only 50% of eligible patients due to manual physician activation. We hypothesized that a multimodal AI model could automate CDS activation by identifying rib fracture patients directly from emergency department (ED) data..
Methods
We developed and externally validated a multimodal AI model integrating ED chest X-rays and EHR data across 28,126 adult patients from four U.S. academic health systems (35 hospitals). The model was trained and internally validated on 21,658 patients (Sites 1 and 2), then externally validated on 5,550 patients (Site 3) and 918 patients (Site 4). Performance was assessed using AUROC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV. Equity was evaluated across racial and gender subgroups using DeLong tests, accuracy parity, and equalized odds. Following validation, the model was deployed to production at M Health Fairview with hourly automated inference.
Results
Internally, the model achieved AUROC 0.90 (95% CI: 0.88-0.91), sensitivity 80%, specificity 83%, and NPV 96% at Site 1, and AUROC 0.80 (95% CI: 0.74-0.85) at Site 2. External AUROC was 0.75 at Site 3 and 0.70 at Site 4. Multimodal fusion outperformed CXR-only by 0.13 AUROC and 0.24 AUPRC. No significant performance differences were found across racial or gender subgroups at any site (all DeLong p > 0.65). The model demonstrated stable performance over three months of production monitoring.
Significance
A multimodal AI model integrating CXR imaging with EHR data accurately identifies rib fracture patients and has been deployed to automate activation of a proven CDS system, representing a scalable mechanism to eliminate the manual bottleneck currently limiting EBP delivery to 50% of eligible patients, with the potential to substantially reduce rib fracture morbidity and mortality at a population level.

Medical Imaging · University of Minnesota · Proposal to Improve Existing Methods
Proposed: Vision-Language Model for Grounded Rib Fracture Reporting
Background
Multimodal deep learning models for rib fracture diagnosis have demonstrated strong discriminative performance, yet remain fundamentally limited: they produce binary probability scores without localizing fractures, cannot generate interpretable clinical reports, and neglect patient-facing communication. Furthermore, they ignore free-text clinical notes: a rich source of contextual information that structured EHR fields cannot capture. I proposed a Vision-Language Model (VLM) that addresses all these limitations simultaneously.
Architecture
The proposed system accepts trimodal input: chest X-rays encoded by a medical vision transformer (BiomedCLIP), 26 structured EHR variables processed by the proven feedforward network from our deployed pipeline, and free-text clinical notes encoded by ClinicalBERT. A LoRA-fine-tuned medical LLM decoder with spatial grounding tokens produces three outputs in a single forward pass: bounding-box-localized fracture identification, a structured physician-facing radiology report, and a plain-language patient summary at a 6th-grade reading level. Visual Question Answering enables natural language clinician queries against the image.
Training Strategy
Training follows a three-stage strategy: CLIP-style vision-language pretraining on MIMIC-CXR and CheXpert (600K+ images), grounded report generation fine-tuning on our 28,126-patient multi-site dataset, and readability optimization via RLHF with Flesch-Kincaid and SMOG reward signals. Evaluation spans diagnostic performance (AUROC, IoU), report quality (RadGraph-F1, hallucination rate, radiologist preference), patient comprehension, and equity across demographic subgroups.
Significance
Designed to integrate with our deployed CDS infrastructure at M Health Fairview, this system represents the first trimodal medical VLM combining localization, dual-audience report generation, and automated clinical decision support activation in a unified architecture.

Medical Imaging · TechMah Medical · Deployed at UTK hospitals
Patient-specific 3D Knee Joint Reconstruction from Orthogonal 2D X-ray Images for Pre-Operative Total Knee Arthroplasty using Deep Learning
Background
Accurate three-dimensional reconstruction of patient-specific knee anatomy is essential for implant selection in total knee arthroplasty. CT-based planning remains the standard but imposes radiation burden, cost, and scheduling constraints. Statistical shape model (SSM) approaches reconstruct bone geometry from standard radiographs at sub-millimeter accuracy, yet depend on manual pose initialization: a specialist-dependent, time-consuming step that introduces operator variability and drives reconstruction failures through poor SSM convergence. We present a fully automated deep learning pipeline that eliminates manual initialization while improving reconstruction accuracy. Two stages operate sequentially
Stage 1
First, a dual-encoder U-Net with GoogLeNet-style bottleneck layers and CheXpert-initialized weights performs four-class segmentation of AP and lateral radiographs into background, femur, tibia, and overlap regions, achieving a boundary RMS of 1.85 mm at 300 ms per image on CPU.
Stage 2
Second, a shallow five-layer CNN regresses 3D bone orientation from binary silhouettes, trained on 280K synthetically rendered projections from 312 CT-derived surface models. Operating on silhouettes rather than raw radiographs removes all confounding intensity variation caused by tissue thickness, bone density, and X-ray dose, reducing pose regression to a low-dimensional geometric problem that does not require deep architectures.
Results and Future Direction
On 25 held-out subjects, femoral reconstruction RMS improved from 1.04±0.33 mm to 0.94±0.24 mm and tibial from 1.03±0.19 mm to 0.93±0.18 mm, with initialization time falling from 5-10 minutes of specialist labor to under two seconds and estimated SSM convergence failures dropping from ~15% to under 2% of frames. The remaining error budget is now attributable to SSM fitting itself, identifying learned generative models as the natural next step toward a fully neural reconstruction pipeline.

Medical Imaging · TechMah Medical · Proposal to Improve Exisiting Methods
Proposed: Replacing Statistical Shape Modeling with a Generative Neural Embedding for 3D Bone Reconstruction
Exisiting Limitations
Statistical shape models (SSMs) for 3D bone reconstruction from 2D radiographs are bounded by three structural limitations: a linearized shape space that struggles to represent fine condylar and articular surface geometry, iterative optimization requiring 18-20 minutes per bone that precludes clinical deployment, and a non-differentiable pipeline that prevents joint end-to-end training.
Proposed Methods
I proposed replacing the KPCA-SSM fitting stage in an existing knee reconstruction pipeline with a TL-embedding network (Girdhar et al., ECCV 2016), a jointly trained architecture that learns a compact latent space satisfying two simultaneous criteria: generative in 3D, such that a decoder can reconstruct a full bone model from a latent vector in a single forward pass; and predictable from 2D, such that a CNN image encoder can map a binary bone silhouette directly to that latent vector without iterative search. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a 3D point cloud autoencoder learns the bone shape manifold from CT-derived surface meshes; second, an image encoder is trained to predict the frozen latent vectors from synthetic 2D silhouette projections, reusing the rendering pipeline already developed for upstream pose estimation.
Inference & Anticipated Performance
At inference, 3D reconstruction reduces to two forward passes- image encoder then decoder- replacing a 20-minute pattern search with sub-second inference. Anticipated reconstruction accuracy matches or exceeds the SSM baseline, with the additional benefits of a smooth and interpolable latent space, natural uncertainty quantification via latent distance, and scalability beyond the fixed 300-model SSM corpus.
Independent Validation
This proposal was presented internally at TechMah Medical in May 2021 and independently validated by Shiode et al. (Scientific Reports, July 2021), who applied an identical architecture to distal forearm reconstruction from single-view X-ray, achieving 1.05 mm RMS for the radius, which is competitive with SSM-based methods at a fraction of the inference cost.
